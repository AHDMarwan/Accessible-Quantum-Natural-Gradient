\section{Accessible tangent geometry and AQNG}
\subsection{Fixed measurement, retained features, and score geometry}
Consider a fixed measurement with outcomes $z\in\{1,\ldots,K\}$ and probability vector $p_{\bm\theta}(x)$ for input $x$. Let
\begin{equation}
 Q=\frac{\partial p_{\bm\theta}(x)}{\partial\bm\theta}\in\mathbb R^{K\times d},
 \qquad D_p=\operatorname{diag}(p_{\bm\theta}(x)).
\end{equation}
On the regular support of $p$, the Fisher-normalized probability tangent matrix is
\begin{equation}
 S=D_p^{-1/2}Q,
\end{equation}
and the classical Fisher information matrix (CFIM) of the complete measurement record is
\begin{equation}
 G_{\mathcal M}=S^{\mathsf T}S=Q^{\mathsf T}D_p^{+}Q.
 \label{eq:measurement_cfim}
\end{equation}
For a pure-state model, $G_{\mathcal M}\preceq G_Q$, with equality only when the chosen measurement preserves the relevant quantum Fisher geometry.

The learning interface generally retains only a collection of commuting outcome functions $f_1(z),\ldots,f_r(z)$. Write $F\in\mathbb R^{K\times r}$ for the matrix with entries $F_{z\alpha}=f_\alpha(z)$, let
\begin{equation}
 \bm\mu=F^{\mathsf T}p,
 \qquad
 \widetilde F=F-\bm 1\bm\mu^{\mathsf T},
\end{equation}
and define the feature covariance and parameter Jacobian
\begin{equation}
 \Sigma=\widetilde F^{\mathsf T}D_p\widetilde F,
 \qquad
 J=\frac{\partial\bm\mu}{\partial\bm\theta}=F^{\mathsf T}Q.
 \label{eq:feature_jac_cov}
\end{equation}
Because probability tangents satisfy $\bm 1^{\mathsf T}Q=0$, one also has $J=\widetilde F^{\mathsf T}Q$.

\subsection{Exact score-projection identity}
Define $B=D_p^{1/2}\widetilde F$. Then $\Sigma=B^{\mathsf T}B$ and $J=B^{\mathsf T}S$. Therefore
\begin{align}
 G_{\rm acc}
 &\equiv J^{\mathsf T}\Sigma^{+}J \\
 &=S^{\mathsf T}B(B^{\mathsf T}B)^{+}B^{\mathsf T}S \\
 &=S^{\mathsf T}P_BS,
 \label{eq:score_projection_identity}
\end{align}
where $P_B$ is the Euclidean projector onto the covariance-weighted retained feature span. Thus, for commuting features derived from a common measurement record, $J^{\mathsf T}\Sigma^{+}J$ is not merely a heuristic approximation to the CFIM: it is the exact Gram matrix of the measurement score after projection onto the retained readout subspace. Consequently,
\begin{equation}
 0\preceq G_{\rm acc}\preceq G_{\mathcal M}\preceq G_Q.
 \label{eq:metric_nesting}
\end{equation}
Rank deficiency is handled by the Moore--Penrose pseudoinverse. In finite-shot experiments we additionally regularize the empirical covariance; that regularized metric should be read as a stabilized estimator of Eq.~\eqref{eq:score_projection_identity}, not as a new exact projector identity.

This construction also clarifies the scope of the method. A list of noncommuting observables does not by itself define a single jointly measurable covariance matrix $\Sigma$; one must first specify a measurement grouping or another explicit experimental interface. The present experiments use diagonal commuting outcome functions derived from computational-basis samples.

\subsection{Accessible quantum natural gradient}
For a metric minibatch $B_G$, AQNG averages the accessible pullbacks,
\begin{equation}
 G_A(\bm\theta)=\frac{1}{|B_G|}\sum_{x\in B_G}J_x^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_x^{+}J_x.
 \label{eq:GA}
\end{equation}
The supervised loss gradient $g=\nabla_{\bm\theta}L$ is shared across optimizer comparisons. AQNG forms a controlled preconditioned direction
\begin{equation}
 d_A=(\widehat G_A+\lambda_{\rm eff}I)^{-1}g,
 \qquad
 \bm\theta\leftarrow\bm\theta-\eta d_A,
 \label{eq:aqng_update}
\end{equation}
where $\widehat G_A$ denotes the chosen metric normalization and $\lambda_{\rm eff}$ the damping convention. The implementation supports trace or maximum-eigenvalue normalization, absolute or relative damping, and both Euclidean and accessible-metric trust controls. The frozen paper-scale protocol uses trace normalization with absolute damping.

The full-QNG baseline instead uses the pure-state QFIM with the explicit convention
\begin{equation}
 (G_Q)_{ij}=4\,\mathrm{Re}\!\left[
 \langle\partial_i\psi|\partial_j\psi\rangle
 -\langle\partial_i\psi|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|\partial_j\psi\rangle
 \right].
 \label{eq:qfim}
\end{equation}
AQNG is therefore not defined by the requirement $G_A\approx G_Q$ in matrix norm. It intentionally changes the geometry by discarding score directions absent from the retained measurement interface.

\subsection{Rank-matched readout orientation}
The retained feature span is calibrated once at the common initial parameter point and then frozen during supervised training. The paper-scale controls use three equal-rank designs: a physical low-weight diagonal readout, a Haar-random rank-matched subspace of the same score space, and a cross-fitted aligned subspace estimated from independent label-free tangent directions. The aligned projector is evaluated on held-out tangents so that its reported retention is not the same-sample Ky Fan optimum.

For a normalized held-out tangent covariance $C$ in a centered score space of dimension $N$ and a rank-$r$ projector $P$, we report
\begin{equation}
 R=\Tr(PC),\qquad \rho=\frac{R}{r/N}.
 \label{eq:retention}
\end{equation}
A Haar-random rank-$r$ subspace has $\mathbb E\rho=1$; deviations of the physical or aligned readout from this baseline diagnose orientation rather than rank. Crucially, $R$ and $\rho$ are geometry diagnostics, not supervised performance metrics. The experiments below test when better tangent accessibility does---and does not---translate into a better optimization trajectory.
